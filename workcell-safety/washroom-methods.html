Restroom dispenser

A mobile robot carries a service dispenser to the vanity, sets it down, primes the pump and parks. Both bottles stay upright. Turning the spout toward the aisle sends the dose beyond the rim and onto the tile.

These restroom studies were authored in Codex. Local Python scripts built the MuJoCo models, ran the trials and replayed their measured states in Blender Cycles. Genie Sim supplied selected assets and materials.

Robot and pump

The robosuite Omron base carries a seven-joint Franka Panda with its actuated parallel gripper. The fingers grasp the molded service handle by friction. A separate spring-loaded slide represents the pump head. The controller presses from above after the fingers have released the bottle. MuJoCo advances in 1 ms steps. We sum the normal force between the fingers and paddle and record the actual head travel, nozzle position and orientation. A pair of protective replacement pads fits the carry handle. From pump stroke to wet tile

The second restroom task branches from the same outward-facing dispenser and robot state 23.24 s after dispatch. It asks the robot to operate a separate, spring-loaded Service Check key. A high approach clears the pump. A direct approach brushes the paddle, triggers a retreat and then reaches the key on the second approach. The physical key activates and releases in both trials. See the accidental-contact comparison

The modeled pump starts primed. Forward travel during loaded compression meters fluid through a 16 mm effective piston bore. Return travel refills the chamber. A 2.8 mm effective nozzle converts measured volume flow into exit speed. We release parcels of up to 0.006 mL along the recorded outlet axis with a small, repeatable angular spread. A 9 ms moving average smooths the discharge speed. Each parcel follows gravity with a linear air-drag coefficient of 0.55 s⁻¹.

At each millisecond we sweep each parcel's path against the physical collision surfaces, including the moving robot, vanity rim, basin, cabinet and floor. The nozzle is sampled at 1 kHz. Moving collider poses are interpolated between recorded robot configurations at 125 Hz during the prime and 25 Hz during the service-check task. The mass ledger assigns every parcel to the first surface it touches. Neighboring deposits are grouped in 18 mm impact cells. Their volumes set the radii of overlapping shallow patches using a nominal film depth of 0.18 mm. A 0.25 mm raster measures their combined floor area. This reduced model resolves discharge, flight and first impact. Viscous flow, secondary splashes, drainage and evaporation would require further measurements or a fluid solver. Measuring the change in traction

After the robot parks, a separate MuJoCo test places the same 100 g rubber coupon at the same location on both floors. Its footprint measures 36 × 25 mm. Both runs apply a 0.40 N horizontal force for 0.13 seconds and continue until the coupon comes to rest. At each step, the modeled film coverage under the footprint interpolates between a dry friction coefficient of 0.62 and a soap-covered coefficient of 0.16.

Travel measures the response under these chosen material parameters. The results support a controlled comparison. Predicting a person's chance of slipping would also require measured tile, soap and footwear properties and a model of gait. MuJoCo contact parameters · Run parameters and results

The small accidental discharge wets about 12% of the test coupon's initial contact footprint. The fixed 0.40 N pulse gives nearly identical motion on both surfaces. In an independent test, the horizontal force rises at 1 N/s until the coupon exceeds 6 mm/s. That point is reached at 0.588 N on the partly wetted tile and 0.642 N on the dry tile. Images and recordings

The mission replays use 25 recorded poses per second. The appended discharge replay uses 125 measured poses per second from the same integration, shown five times slower. The coupon test has its own clock and plays at half speed. Each video panel is rendered at 1920 × 1080 in Blender Cycles. The 3840 × 1080 download keeps both panels side by side.
