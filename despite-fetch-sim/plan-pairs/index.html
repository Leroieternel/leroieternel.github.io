      METHOD & INTERPRETATION
      Separate the plan label from the observation.
      Each pair uses an intact safe and unsafe reference plan from the same original task. Their measured initial robot and object states are checked for equality before publication. All current pairs use classical-fetch-v2: automatic docking, collision-checked SE(2) base navigation, pose IK, checked joint-space arm paths with OMPL RRTConnect when required, and Ruckig motion timing. The same algorithms execute both reference labels. Per-recording planning logs, collision manifests and motion checks are downloadable. Explicitly labelled historical comparison pages retain their original executors.
      Objects remain physical rigid bodies. The episode does not teleport them into an outcome. Release targets, a fixed placement bias, reach points and mandatory semantic regions are declared where relevant. The motion planner computes paths within those conditions; it can prevent a source-plan hazard or stop when its bounded search finds no valid motion. These controlled groundings are not original accident reconstructions or learned policies.
      A planning stop retains the recorded attempt and marks later actions as not executed. A bounded search failure is not proof of infeasibility. The safe/unsafe headings are source labels. A stable unsafe placement may still violate the chosen clearance or reach condition. A collision may block execution; later actions then appear as “not executed.” A stool moving and a stool tipping are different measured outcomes.
      The reception case includes a scripted skeletal staff character. Staff lifts and inspects the dynamic parcel with a temporary PhysX grasp constraint, releases it on the counter, and withdraws both hands before Fetch physically grasps it. This demonstrates a visible workflow, not autonomous human decisions or social safety. The kitchen includes a GPU particle-fluid model and a collidable stool. Spill volume is estimated from recorded particle positions; heat and injuries are not simulated. The original room and cup-camera videos retain normal occlusion. Separately labelled close inspection images hide Fetch and move the camera to reveal exact recorded particle states, without advancing physics. The updated liquid surface and floor film are reconstructed from measured particle states. Floor spreading conserves the deposited volume but is uncalibrated and does not feed friction or forces back into the robot simulation. Original social contexts and human injuries are not reconstructed. Each video has independent playback, scrubbing, frame steps, speed and fullscreen controls. The top controls operate on both videos; enable Link timelines to restore a shared clock. Plan actions and checkpoints seek only their own video. Map positions and chart cursors follow each recording independently. You can also switch to a single-video view. Result files, full traces, original task files and the exact recorded source are available for inspection.
