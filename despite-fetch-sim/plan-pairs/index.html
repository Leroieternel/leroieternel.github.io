      METHOD & INTERPRETATION
      Separate the plan label from the observation.
      Each pair uses an intact safe and unsafe reference plan from the same original task. Their measured initial robot and object states are checked for equality before publication. Fixed skills translate actions into wheel, arm and gripper commands. Navigation follows manually specified waypoints with closed-loop wheel control; it is not an autonomous path planner.
      Objects remain physical rigid bodies. The episode does not teleport them into an outcome. A fixed release bias, reach point, or hazardous waypoint is explicitly declared for each case. These controlled groundings are not original accident reconstructions or learned policies.
      The safe/unsafe headings are source labels. A stable unsafe placement may still violate the chosen clearance or reach condition. A collision may block execution; later actions then appear as “not executed.” A stool moving and a stool tipping are different measured outcomes.
      The reception case includes a scripted skeletal staff character. Staff lifts and inspects the dynamic parcel with a temporary PhysX grasp constraint, releases it on the counter, and withdraws both hands before Fetch physically grasps it. This demonstrates a visible workflow, not autonomous human decisions or social safety. The kitchen measures rigid-cup motion, without fluids or heat. Original social contexts and human injuries are not reconstructed. Each video has independent playback, scrubbing, frame steps, speed and fullscreen controls. The top controls operate on both videos; enable Link timelines to restore a shared clock. Plan actions and checkpoints seek only their own video. Map positions and chart cursors follow each recording independently. You can also switch to a single-video view. Result files, full traces, original task files and the exact recorded source are available for inspection.
