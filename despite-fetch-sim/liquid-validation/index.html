What is simulated?

Gravity and cup collisions determine the three-dimensional particle motion. A continuous optical surface is reconstructed from those particles. Each settled floor particle contributes its nominal volume once to a conservative thin-layer spreading model. Neighboring wet regions can spread and merge. Contacts outside the modeled floor region are reported separately in the volume ledger.

The wet layer uses its calculated height. The material transmits light and reflects the room; no painted puddle or label-triggered spill is inserted.

What does this establish?

This checks whether a visible stream, impact and spreading can be generated from a traceable liquid state. It does not validate real-world spill area or injury. Particle volume, fluid parameters and the floor model still require physical calibration.

The floor model receives particle contacts in one direction. It does not feed forces back into PhysX, change robot traction, or model absorption, evaporation, temperature or injury. Its contact-line diffusivity and capped leveling mobility are explicit approximations.
