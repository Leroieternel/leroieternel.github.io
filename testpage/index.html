Abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have become a powerful foundation for robotic scene understanding, task planning, and language-conditioned control. A natural question follows: can these models also decode what a human operator intends while teleoperating a robot, without any explicit instruction? We formulate this as hierarchical intent prediction, jointly inferring the ongoing atomic action and the broader mission it serves from a short visual teleoperation history.

To study this, we introduce VI AMO (Vision Intention Action and Mission Ongoing), a dataset of 6,600 episodes from eleven robot sources with 13,653 temporally aligned action annotations and 9,702 mission annotations. We find that large proprietary models struggle with this problem: on test episodes, GPT-4o reaches 0.570 and 0.476 Sentence-BERT similarity for action and mission, and GPT-5.6 Luna 0.588 and 0.562. Fine-tuning a VLM on VI AMO yields our Vision-Intention Model (VIM), which raises both to 0.824 and still reaches 0.736 and 0.741 on more than one third of test actions and missions whose labels never appear in training.

Finally, as a downstream application, VIM’s predicted mission lets a vision-language-action (VLA) policy take over from the operator mid-task in a real-world environment unseen in training, reducing human teleoperation time across five real-world tasks by 34.6% relative to GPT-4o and 39.8% relative to Luna, with the latency reduced by approximately 70% on average.

Intent Prediction Results

Prediction from partial observations

Across nine observation cutoffs spanning 0–95% of an action, VIM achieves higher action and mission similarity than the six baselines under both SBERT and SigLIP. Additional visual history generally improves predictions.

Generalization to unseen labels

On test actions for which both action and mission labels are absent from training, VIM reaches 0.736 action SBERT and 0.741 mission SBERT. This evaluation measures unseen label strings within the dataset’s source mixture.

Real-world task quantitative results

Across five real-world tasks, VIM reduces mean human teleoperation time by 34.6% relative to GPT-4o and 39.8% relative to GPT-5.6 Luna.

Mean VIM inference latency ranges from 0.70 to 0.81 seconds across tasks. The plots report mean ± standard deviation.
